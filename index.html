Smart Home (IoT) Cybersecurity Risk Research

Cybersecurity Risk Analysis on Smart Home devices.

Tech Stack

  • Articles
  • Coursework
  • Google Slides/Docs

Project Log

Researched the cybersecurity risks created from owning smart home devices. The topics covered in the research, in the form of a Risk Report using the SUCCESS framework, consisted of Governance, Access Control, Asset Management, Data Security, Human Resources, Software Security, Incident Response, Vulnerability Management, Risk Management, Physical Security, and the Team. In a brief explanation, we found that the security goals and priorities lie with both the producer and consumer as the liklihood of these IoT technologies connected to the homes' internet is increasing, allowing ease of access upon a breach. Keeping an updated inventory and understanding of the risks of each device becomes vital as more technologies, such as Alexa's, acquire data, whether sensitive or not. In terms of software, consumers should keep applications up to date and favor companies that provide long-term security measures, such as software encryption, and not share sensitive information, such as passwords, and in doing so will also allow for vulnerability management. These devices can also be physically at risk and should have limited physical access points. The team, including myself, involved in this research and presentation, won the Undergraduate Architecture category at Texas A&M's Student Research Week.

Digital Forensic Investigator Mock Expert Report

Conducted a Digital Forensic Investigation on a suspect and wrote a mock expert report.

Tech Stack

  • Steganography Decoder/Encoder
  • Terminal
  • SQLitebrowser
  • Disk Image Mounter
  • Wireshark
  • Kali Linux VM

Project Log

Imitated an investigator a part of the Naval Criminal Investigative Service (NCIS) to conduct a digital forensic analysis on a seized laptop. A forensic image of the suspects laptop was provided, and was recalculated to verify the MD5 hash matched the given value. We found Discord, FireFox, a Steganography application, and TrueCrypt downloaded on his laptop. We started with Discord uncovering chats between the suspect and other individuals about drugs, suppliers, blackmail and threats, and sharing of Personal Identification Information for a flight. This led us to search his FireFox search history using SQLitebrowser to ensure accuracy of the data. We found flights to Australia, concealment methods of cocaine, image steganography, and encryption methods. Upon further investigation of his laptop, in the Documents Folder, we found a folder called "Business" where an image, a shipping label with all information of suspect, and a spreadsheet of clients existed. This spreadsheet contained names that connected to Discord messages. We also found drug memes, a picture of a victim's kids, a picture of the drugs, and file named "secret". Ultimately, our evidence supported the theory that the suspect was a part of a drug ring operation in Australia.
